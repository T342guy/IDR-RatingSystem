# Test Media

## D and G media

| | D medium | G medium |
|---|---|---|
| **Home kit** | Calcium carbonate powder, passing the #200 sieve (< 75 µm) | Silica (quartz) sand, such as play sand that passes the [carbonate check](#carbonate-check), sieved into two fractions and blended 50/50 by mass: 150–425 µm (passes #40, held on #100) and 425–850 µm (passes #20, held on #40) |
| **Lab kit** | ISO 12103-1 A1–A2 blend | Same sieved blend as the home kit, from graded silica test sand |
| **Preparation** | Store sealed; use as supplied after sieving | Oven-dry at 105–110 °C for 1 hour; cool before use |
| **Reuse** | Discard after each full test | Re-sieve before reuse |

> **Note (informative):** D and G use different materials because sealing (D) depends mainly on particle *size*, so a soft, low-hazard mineral like calcium carbonate is a reasonable home substitute. Mechanism damage (G) depends on particle *hardness* as well, so G uses real quartz sand.

## Carbonate check

Home-kit sand that is not sold as silica sand shall pass this check before use.

1. Drop a pinch of the sand into white vinegar.
2. Watch for a steady stream of bubbles.
3. Sand that produces a steady stream of bubbles shall not be used.

> **Note (informative):** Some play sand is crushed limestone, marble or coral, which is calcium carbonate. It fizzes in vinegar and is much softer than quartz, so it would understate grit damage.

## Ultrafine medium

A home tester who wants an ultrafine D medium (< 10 µm) shall buy a graded powder and record its datasheet particle size in the report.

> **Note (informative):** Particles finer than 75 µm cannot be produced with hand sieves.

## UV tracer

When a UV tracer is used, about 1% UV-fluorescent pigment by mass shall be mixed into the D medium. It is optional at the home tier and required for lab-tier D tests (see [D: Dust](../groups/dust.md#medium)).

The water used for W is defined in [W: Water](../groups/water.md#medium). Handling safety for all media is in [Safety](../testing/safety.md).
